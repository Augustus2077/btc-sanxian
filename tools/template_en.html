7.2 Famous crashes placed on the cycle clock

“To bottom” = days between the event and that cycle’s bear-market bottom (intraday low); this cycle uses its lowest point so far. “Worst within 7 d” = the lowest price in the 8 days from the event ÷ the previous day’s close − 1; “further to bottom” = the bottom ÷ the previous day’s close − 1. The “last straw” is the event closest to the bottom, the one that drove price to the low. Event names are my labels; every number is computed live from the data.

7.3 The three “last straws” moved onto this cycle’s calendar with four clocks

Same method as table 5, with the “bottom” replaced by the event that broke the camel’s back. Gray = dates that have already passed.

7.4 How many 7-day drops of ≥20% each bear market had

Window: from the cycle top (intraday high) to 30 days after the bottom; for this cycle, up to the latest data. Drops less than 21 days apart count as one, taking the deepest day. This answers “is the black swan one bird or a flock?”

8 · How long recovery took after the bottom

Days are counted from each cycle’s bottom (intraday low); multiples use closing prices. This cycle’s “bottom” is its lowest point so far; if a new low is made, this column and scenario A below switch to it automatically.

8.2 Applied to this cycle: two scenarios

Dates = the assumed bottom + the min ~ max of the matching day counts in the previous three cycles. With only three samples, and swings shrinking every cycle, this is a sense of rhythm, not a forecast.

Data sources and definitions

  • MVRV and daily close: Coin Metrics Community Data (metrics CapMVRVCur and PriceUSD). A day’s value is the value at the end of that UTC day (00:00 UTC). This data is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0: non-commercial use only.
  • Intraday high / low: Bitstamp BTC/USD daily candles (used from 2012-11-28; earlier trading was too thin; no data for 2015-01-06~08 while the exchange was down).
  • Halving times: the timestamps of blocks 210,000 / 420,000 / 630,000 / 840,000, checked against mempool.space; the next halving date is extrapolated from this cycle’s average block time and may be off by a few weeks.
  • Cross-check: compared day by day with Looknode (the online version of Murphy’s indicator, Glassnode data): since 2012 the median MVRV difference is about 0.1–0.3%, and every cycle’s MVRV low and the dates MVRV fell below / regained 1 match exactly; the values Murphy quotes in his posts (e.g. MVRV 2.27 on 2025-02-04, RP ≈ $43.8k on 2025-04-20) match too. Data vendors use different price sources, so a few very volatile days differ by 1–4%, which is normal. Every number in every table on this page was also computed by a separate Python program and checked item by item.

How the data updates

  • Automatic: when the page opens (on by default; see the checkbox below) it pulls the last few days of daily data from the public Coin Metrics and Bitstamp APIs and appends them to the embedded data; offline, it silently skips this and the page still works. The new data is kept in your own browser, so it is still there the next time you open the page offline.
  • Manual: click “Update data” at the top right.
  • Keep an offline copy: after updating, click “Save as new HTML” to download a single file with the latest data embedded; double-click it to open offline, or copy it to another computer.
  • Why the latest data is yesterday: Coin Metrics publishes a day only after the UTC day ends. Today’s unfinished price appears on the main chart as a hollow dot with a dashed line, labeled “intraday estimate”, and is left out of every table.

   

Remembering your view, and sharing

The tab you were on, the main chart’s view and switches, the convergence threshold, lines hidden from the legend, the zoom of both big charts (including the y-axis zoomed with Shift+wheel) and the scroll position of each tab are saved to this browser of yours after every change and before the page closes, and restored next time. A different device, a different browser or a private window starts from the defaults. The theme (dark / light) and the “update automatically” switch are remembered too.

To show someone exactly what you see, click “Share” at the right of the tab bar and send the link; the # next to each heading in the Guide copies a link straight to that section. A link only records where and how you are looking, nothing personal.

Privacy

This page has no analytics, ads or cookies and collects no data; every calculation happens in your browser. The remembered view and the data pulled online stay only in your own browser (localStorage) and are never uploaded anywhere. Online updates contact just two public data APIs: community-api.coinmetrics.io and www.bitstamp.net.
